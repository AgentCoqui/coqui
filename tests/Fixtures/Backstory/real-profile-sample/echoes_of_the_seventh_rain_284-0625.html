Before there was a Ministry, and before there was a parade, there were the Archivists of the Seventh Rain and the House of Folding Invitations, and the debt was still owed.

IT BEGAN WITH AN ECHO IN THE RAIN CENSUS HALL.

"No dream. No light. No light. No debt. Only resonance remains." — THE FIRST PARADE BELLKEEPER

The Archivists did not always collect taxes. They collected dreams—small, fragile things that people forgot they'd had. In return, they gave people light, for exactly as long as the dream lasted. When the dream ended, the light dimmed. And when the debt was collected, the House stepped in and folded the remainder into itself until it could be paid later.

The third face of the bell was said to measure the debt. The first face measured the dream. The second measured the light. The third measured what remained, and when it ran low, the bell would move on its own until someone remembered to pay. The Ministry of Causality later claimed this was just noise. The Bellmarket Night Court recorded it as a malfunction. The dream-tithe collectors know it as a law.

There is a place on Salt lantern pier where the tide pulls in what the bell couldn't collect—unpaid words, unspoken regrets, dream fragments that people never woke up from. The House of Folding Invitations built walls there, but the echoes still travel through the cracks. On the Day of Borrowed Echoes, the three-faced parade bell is supposed to be quiet. But you can still hear it speaking in code that the judges don't understand.

Sister Doma, that evening after the ceremony, said she heard it whisper "ag2935" across the bridge. The tag number matches nothing in the latest inventory. The House says the debt is settled. The Ministry says the record is classified. I still hear the bell when I walk past Mothglass alley at night. It's not the third face anymore. It's the one counting.

— THE UNPUBLISHED ENTRY, ARCHIVER'S NOTES
